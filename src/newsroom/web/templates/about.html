About

This site lists headlines from a set of news outlets and shows who owns each outlet and how it's funded. Every ownership and funding detail links to the public record it comes from. Nothing is rated, scored or guessed.

What the labels mean

No record found
None of the sources this site checks has a record of this, so nothing is shown. It says nothing about the outlet: the information may well be public elsewhere.
No owner listed on Wikidata
The outlet has a Wikidata entry, but it doesn't list an owner. This is common for independent and nonprofit outlets, but the entry may also be incomplete.
Owned by / Parent organization
Two different Wikidata statements, shown as recorded. "Parent organization" means the organization this one belongs to, which isn't always an owner: a Crown corporation's parent organization is a government department. A percentage appears only when the record gives one.
Shareholder
A stake under half, as the record states it. A shareholder isn't an owner. For a publicly traded company, the one-line summary stops there, because it's owned by its shareholders.
"whose owner is" / "whose parent organization is"
The chain above the direct owner, one record at a time, as far as the records go. The outlet page shows the whole chain.
source
Each record links to where it comes from. Hover over the link to see the record and the date it was retrieved.
Published / Seen
Published is the time given on the article page. Seen is when the article was first picked up. It's shown when the page gives no date, and can be later than the real publication time.

Articles

Headlines come from the GDELT Project and, for outlets GDELT doesn't carry, from the outlet's own RSS feed. Both are checked every {{ interval }} minutes. Only the headline, link, outlet, language and date are kept, never the article text. Links go straight to the outlet. Times are in {{ request.app.state.settings.timezone }}. A few outlets are in neither source; their page says so.

Some outlets publish far more than others, often because they also run wire stories. So the feed shows at most {{ cap }} articles per outlet per hour, and tells you how many it left out. It also spaces outlets out, so the same one doesn't keep coming back: where there's enough variety, an outlet appears at most once in any four stories in a row. To do that a story may move a few places, never by more than half an hour. Choose Show: Everything to see them all. An outlet's own page and the outlet filter always show everything.

Topic tags

Tags come from two places. First, GDELT's coding of the article's full text: GDELT tags each article with themes (such as elections or climate change), and a tag is added when its themes come up several times, not just in passing. Second, for articles that doesn't tag, the outlet's own section or topic labels from the article page (such as "Health" or "Politique"). Hover over a tag to see where it came from. Tags describe what an article covers, not a judgement about it.

Country and search

GDELT also reads where each story takes place and who it's about. A country counts when it comes up at least twice and makes up a good share of the places mentioned, so a passing mention doesn't make a story about Paris. The Country filter uses that; "European Union" covers stories about any of its 27 members. Search looks at the headline and at the people and countries a story is about, so searching a name finds stories about that person even when the headline doesn't use the name. In results, the matching part of each headline is marked; when the match isn't in the headline, a "Matched:" line shows the name it was found under. The last word you type also matches longer words, so "mark" finds Markets as well as Mark Carney. Articles that only come from an outlet's own feed have none of this, so they're found by their headline only.

Dates

The site checks each new article's page for its publication date, the same date search engines use. (RSS feeds also list dates, but some get the time zone wrong, so they aren't used.) Only the date is kept. New articles appear once their date has been checked, usually within a minute of being collected, so they show the right time from the start. Each page is checked at most twice, and each site's robots.txt is respected.

A date isn't used if it's later than when the article was first picked up, or if the page's own date tags contradict each other about the time zone. Those articles show when they were seen instead.

Ownership

Each outlet is matched to its Wikidata entry by its official website. When several entries list that website, the one Wikidata classifies as a news outlet is used; when none does, a search by name is accepted only if the result's website is the outlet's own. The outlet page says how each match was made. From there the site follows "owned by" and "parent organization" upward, up to ten levels, and stops at a person (people aren't owned). Only current records are used: not ones with an end date, and not an owner who is a person who has since died. Ownership is rechecked weekly.

Wikidata is sometimes missing an owner or behind on a sale. Those gaps are filled from a cited public source (a company announcement, a news report), marked "correction" with the source linked. An outdated Wikidata record is set aside the same way: the outlet page says what was set aside and why, and it's never shown as current.

Funding

Shown only where a public record exists:

  • Public companies: their latest annual report and filings on SEC EDGAR. No figures are taken from these, because a company's revenue isn't funding.
  • US nonprofits: revenue and donations from IRS Form 990 filings, via ProPublica.
  • Canadian charities: their listing with the Canada Revenue Agency.
  • Public broadcasters and others: figures as published in annual reports or government documents.

Where none of these has a record, the page says so; that isn't a claim that the outlet hides its funding.

What this site doesn't do

  • No bias or reliability ratings, and no scores of any kind.
  • No commentary.
  • No accounts, tracking, analytics or cookies. Your browser only connects to this site.
  • No article text or images.
